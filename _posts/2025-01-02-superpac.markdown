This study examines how Super PACs reshapes minority representation in U.S. local elections after the 2010 Citizens United ruling. Using a new dataset of over 43,000 local contests across 20 years and a difference-in-differences two-way fixed effects design, the paper shows that Super PACs significantly reduce the presence, entry, and electoral success of minority candidates—defined as female and non-White candidates—especially in nonpartisan races. Heterogeneity analyses reveal that non-White Democrats experience the largest declines, while female Republicans see modest gains. The results underscore how outside spending can exacerbate representational inequalities and complicate efforts to achieve equitable participation in local democracy.
